FAQ

What simUT computes — and what it does not

What does simUT compute today?
simUT computes the emitted ultrasonic beam field in 2D and 3D. It does not yet simulate a full inspection.
Which probe and coupling setups are available?
Single-element and phased array probes, in contact and in immersion, on flat parts. A wedge coupling is also available in the desktop app.
Can it simulate an inspection or a defect?
Not today. Sensitivity maps and inspection simulation are on the roadmap, and they are not presented as available.
Are curved geometries supported?
Not yet. Curved geometries are planned and marked as coming.
What can I export?
From the results view, a field map can be exported as a PNG image or as CSV data. Studies can be saved and opened again.
What platform does it run on?
simUT is a desktop application for Windows.
Who builds simUT?
simUT is developed by ROBINSPECT SYSTEMS, LLC. It was started by Vincent Pretet, an NDT engineer who has worked in the field since 2019, mostly in the nuclear industry.
Do I need simulation training to use simUT?
No. simUT is designed to be easy to pick up, so engineers and technicians can prepare an inspection without specialist simulation training. Knowing UT and your probe is enough to get started.
What comes after beam field computation?
Sensitivity maps come next, then inspection simulation, then curved geometries. After that, the roadmap follows user needs and feedback. We don't publish dates.
How do I get access?
Join the beta. We will reach out when the next build is ready.

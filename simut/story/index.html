simUT/Story
Story & direction

Why we built simUT, and where it's going

simUT is ultrasonic beam simulation software for NDT, built by ROBINSPECT SYSTEMS, LLC. It began as a field engineer's answer to a simple problem: simulation should help you prepare an inspection, not slow you down.

Where simUT comes from

Vincent Pretet, Founder, ROBINSPECT SYSTEMS, LLC. NDT engineer since 2019, mostly in the nuclear industry.

Vincent Pretet has worked in NDT since 2019, mostly in the nuclear industry. In 2022 his work moved into phased array UT, and with it into imaging methods, simulation and the post-processing of raw data.

That's where the friction started. Every inspection had to be prepared, every project had to be justified, and every result had to be understood. Simulation was the right tool for all three, but it rarely felt like one. Computations were slow to optimize, the software was hard to drive, and getting a usable answer took more time than it should. simUT started from that frustration.

The problem: simulation that stays out of reach

The need for UT simulation keeps growing. Teams use it to prepare inspections, to scope and defend projects, and to understand what they actually see in the data.

Existing solutions are complete, but they're also complex and expensive. They assume a trained specialist at the keyboard and a budget to match. That leaves a large part of the market without access: smaller inspection companies, technicians who need an answer rather than a modelling project, and engineers who simply want to check a beam before they go on site.

simUT is meant for them, as well as for experienced simulation users who want a faster, more direct tool.

How simUT is built

simUT isn't a new interface on top of old code. It's a completely new engine, designed from the start around a few principles.

  • Built for parallel computing. The architecture uses native multithreading, and computations can run on the GPU. The aim is simple: less waiting between a change in the setup and a result you can read.
  • Easy to pick up. The setup is laid out step by step, so you don't need specialist training to get to a first beam field.
  • Honest about its scope. simUT shows what the model computes and what it doesn't. A beam field map isn't a full inspection simulation, and the software says so.
  • Checked before it ships. No module leaves beta until its results have been checked against independent references.

Where simUT is going

Today, simUT computes the ultrasonic beam field for single-element and phased array probes, in contact, in immersion and with a wedge, on flat parts.

The modules that follow build on that field computation, in this order:

  1. Sensitivity maps
  2. Inspection simulation
  3. Curved geometries

What comes after that will depend on what users need and what they tell us. We're not putting dates on any of it. Each module ships once it's ready.

How to read it

Four acts, one workflow.

The point is the chain: the geometry drives the trajectory, the trajectory drives the robot, and the scan lands on the part as a map. Nobody teaches the robot point by point.

01 · The part

Your geometry is the input

A curved, stiffened composite panel with a cutout. In a real project the input is your CAD file or a 3D scan of the part.

Input · CAD or 3D scan
02 · The trajectory

Computed, not taught

The scan path is computed from the surface: it follows the curvature, keeps the probe normal to the skin and lifts over the cutout.

Shown in the lab demo
03 · The scan

Contact UT on the path

Any robot brand: the arm carries a contact UT probe along the path. Painting the C-scan onto the part is an illustration.

Contact UT · shown in the lab demo
04 · The result

A map, then a report

Indications are located on the part and summarized for the inspector's review. Automated reporting is a direction, not a live product.
